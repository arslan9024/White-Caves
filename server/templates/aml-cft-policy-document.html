1. INTRODUCTION & PURPOSE

White Caves Real Estate LLC ("the Company") is committed to preventing the use of its real estate transaction services for money laundering (ML), financing of terrorism (FT), and proliferation financing. This Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Financing of Terrorism Policy ("the Policy") establishes the company-wide compliance framework to ensure adherence to all applicable AML/CFT laws, regulations, and guidelines issued by the UAE government and regulatory authorities.

1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this Policy is to:

  • Establish clear AML/CFT responsibilities and procedures within White Caves Real Estate LLC
  • Ensure compliance with Federal Decree Law No (20) of 2018 and Cabinet Decision No (10) of 2019
  • Protect the company from regulatory penalties, reputational damage, and involvement in illicit activities
  • Implement Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) procedures
  • Monitor transactions for suspicious activity and file timely reports with the UAE FIU
  • Maintain proper documentation and record-keeping practices
  • Provide mandatory AML/CFT training to all staff members
  • Conduct periodic internal audits and compliance reviews

1.2 Scope

This Policy applies to all employees, agents, authorized representatives, and contractors of White Caves Real Estate LLC. All staff members must understand and comply with the procedures outlined herein.

1.3 Effective Date & Review

This Policy is effective as of {{effectiveDate}} and will be reviewed annually or when regulatory changes occur. The next scheduled review is {{nextReviewDate}}.

2. LEGAL FRAMEWORK & REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

2.1 Governing Laws & Regulations

White Caves Real Estate LLC operates under the following legal framework:

  • Federal Decree Law No (20) of 2018 on Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism
  • Cabinet Decision No (10) of 2019 implementing the AML/CFT law
  • Administrative Decision No (1005) of 2019 on Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Financing of Terrorism Procedures and Guidelines
  • DFSA Rulebook & General Prudential Rules (GPR) for financial institutions
  • AMLR Rules (Anti-Money Laundering Rules) for reporting entities
  • UAE FIU Guidelines & Directives on goAML portal procedures
  • DED (Department of Economic Development) Guidelines on Commercial Licensees

2.3 Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with AML/CFT laws exposes White Caves Real Estate LLC to serious penalties, including:

  • Administrative fines up to AED 1,000,000 for natural persons and up to AED 5,000,000 for entities
  • Suspension or revocation of commercial license
  • Criminal prosecution of responsible individuals
  • Seizure and forfeiture of suspicious assets

  • Severe reputational damage

4. CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD)

4.1 CDD Timing & Triggers

Customer Due Diligence must be performed:

  • Before accepting a new customer for property transactions
  • When updating customer information
  • When suspicious activity is identified
  • Periodically (at least every 3 years) for existing customers

4.2 CDD Information Requirements

For all new customers, the following information must be collected and verified:

Information Individuals Legal Entities Verification Method
Full Legal Name Yes Yes ID / Business License
Date of Birth Yes N/A ID / Passport
Nationality Yes N/A Passport / ID
Occupation / Business Type Yes Yes Customer Statement
Source of Funds Yes Yes Bank Statements / Business Records
Beneficial Owners (>25%) N/A Yes Ownership Documentation
Residential Address Yes Yes Recent Utility Bills / Lease

4.3 CDD Risk Assessment

Based on the information collected, each customer is assigned a risk classification:

  • LOW RISK: Domestic individuals, established businesses, transparent source of funds
  • MEDIUM RISK: Foreign nationals, shell companies, complex ownership structures
  • HIGH RISK: PEPs, sanctioned jurisdictions, cash-based transactions, complex beneficial ownership

4.4 CDD Documentation

All CDD information must be documented in the standardized CDD Form and attached documents must include:

  • Copy of valid identification (passport, ID, business license)
  • Address verification (utility bill not older than 3 months)
  • Proof of source of funds (bank statements, employment letter, business records)
  • Beneficial ownership documentation (for entities)
  • Risk assessment conclusion and officer sign-off

CRITICAL: No customer transaction shall proceed without completed and approved CDD. Bypassing CDD procedures is grounds for immediate disciplinary action.

5. ENHANCED DUE DILIGENCE (EDD) FOR HIGH-RISK CUSTOMERS

5.1 EDD Triggers

Enhanced Due Diligence must be performed for customers meeting any of the following criteria:

  • Transaction value exceeds AED 5,000,000
  • Customer is a Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or family member
  • Customer is from a high-risk or FATF grey-listed jurisdiction
  • Customer has complex beneficial ownership or structures
  • Cash-based transactions or unusual payment methods
  • Customer refuses to provide standard CDD information
  • Previously identified as suspicious or involved in legal issues

5.2 EDD Procedures

For each high-risk customer, the Compliance Officer must:

  • Conduct enhanced background checks using international databases
  • Perform PEP screening against OFAC, UN, and DFSA lists
  • Verify source of funds with independent documentation
  • Conduct adverse media search
  • Interview customer to understand transaction purpose
  • Obtain additional documentation if necessary
  • Document all findings in the EDD Report
  • Obtain explicit approval from Managing Director before proceeding

5.3 PEP Definition & Screening

A Politically Exposed Person (PEP) is an individual who holds or has held a high-ranking public office in the last 12 months, including:

  • Government ministers and senior officials
  • Military or law enforcement leaders
  • Heads of state-owned enterprises
  • Senior judicial officers
  • Members of parliament / legislative bodies
  • Close family members of PEPs
  • Business associates of PEPs

All new customers must be screened against the following lists:

  • UAE Federal Authority for Identity & Citizenship PEP list
  • US OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list
  • UN Security Council Sanctions lists
  • EU Consolidated Sanctions list
  • UK OFSI Sanctions list
  • DFSA Enforcement Directorate lists

Screening must be documented with the name, list checked, and screening result recorded in the CDD file.

6. TRANSACTION MONITORING & SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY

6.1 Transaction Monitoring Framework

All property transactions shall be monitored for indicators of money laundering or terrorism financing. The Compliance Officer will maintain a transaction monitoring log documenting:

  • Customer name and identification
  • Transaction date, amount, and type
  • Property details (location, value, purpose)
  • Source of funds documentation
  • Risk assessment score
  • Monitoring result (cleared, referred, flagged)

6.2 Red Flags & Suspicious Indicators

Staff should watch for the following red flags that may indicate suspicious activity:

  • Transaction Patterns: Unusual transaction amount, frequency, or timing; splitting transactions to avoid detection; cash payments for high-value properties
  • Customer Behavior: Reluctance to provide required information; contradictory or inconsistent statements; transaction structure appears to lack commercial logic
  • Financial Indicators: Funds from countries with weak AML controls; payment through multiple intermediaries; use of shell companies or offshore entities
  • Relationship Indicators: Third-party involvement without clear business reason; family members or associates purchasing properties simultaneously
  • Property-Specific: Overvaluation or undervaluation of property; purchase for immediate resale; property location in sensitive area; overpayment or unusual payment terms

6.3 Reporting Suspicious Activity Internally

Any employee who identifies suspicious activity must immediately report it to the AML Compliance Officer without disclosing suspicions to the customer. Failure to report is a serious compliance breach.

Protection for Reporters: White Caves Real Estate LLC prohibits any retaliation against employees who report suspicious activity in good faith. Whistleblower protections are in place under company policy.

7. REPORTING SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTIONS

7.1 STR Filing Obligation

When the Compliance Officer identifies a transaction that is suspected to be related to money laundering or terrorism financing, a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) must be filed with the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) via the goAML portal within five (5) business days of identification.

7.2 STR Content & Documentation

Each STR must include:

  • Complete customer identification and CDD information
  • Transaction details (date, amount, type, parties involved)
  • Detailed description of suspicious activity and red flags observed
  • Analysis of why activity is suspected to be ML/FT-related
  • All supporting documentation (CDD forms, transaction records, communications)
  • Officer signature and date of report

7.3 Post-Reporting Actions

After filing an STR:

  • The transaction shall not proceed without explicit direction from UAE FIU
  • The Compliance Officer shall continue monitoring the customer for further suspicious activity
  • No staff member shall disclose the filing of an STR to the customer (tipping off prohibited)
  • Documentation shall be retained in the compliance vault for minimum 5 years
  • Internal notification to Senior Management required (without customer awareness)

7.4 Prohibition on "Tipping Off"

IT IS STRICTLY PROHIBITED to inform a customer that an STR has been filed or that the company suspects them of involvement in money laundering or terrorism financing. Doing so is a criminal offense under UAE law and can result in imprisonment and fines. Employees found "tipping off" will face immediate termination and criminal prosecution.

7.5 Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs)

If not applicable to real estate transactions, this section may be omitted. However, if the company handles deposits or currency exchanges, the following applies:

Any deposit transaction exceeding AED 10,000 must be documented. If multiple deposits by the same customer within a 5-day period total over AED 10,000, a Currency Transaction Report should be filed.

8. RECORD KEEPING & DOCUMENTATION

8.1 Retention Period

All AML/CFT-related documentation must be retained for a minimum of five (5) years from the date of the transaction or the end of the customer relationship, whichever is later.

8.2 Records to Be Maintained

  • Customer identification documents (passport, ID, business license copies)
  • Completed CDD and EDD forms with risk assessment
  • Address verification documents
  • Source of funds documentation
  • Beneficial ownership information for entities
  • PEP screening results
  • Transaction records (contracts, payment receipts, wire transfer documents)
  • Communications with customers regarding transaction
  • Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) and STRs filed
  • Internal compliance approvals and sign-offs
  • Staff training certificates and attendance records
  • Internal audit reports and findings
  • Compliance Officer monthly reports

8.3 Record Storage & Security

All compliance records shall be:

  • Stored in a secure, climate-controlled facility (physical records)
  • Maintained in an encrypted digital system with restricted access (digital records)
  • Protected from unauthorized access, alteration, or destruction
  • Organized by customer and transaction for easy retrieval during audits or regulatory inquiries
  • Backed up regularly with copies stored off-site

8.4 Inspection & Audit Access

Records must be available for inspection by:

  • UAE FIU during compliance examinations
  • DED inspectors during commercial license audits
  • External auditors performing compliance reviews
  • Internal auditors and Compliance Officer
  • Senior Management as part of governance oversight

Records should be organized and indexed to allow rapid retrieval during inspections. Any request for records from regulatory authorities should be immediately escalated to Senior Management and documented.

9. STAFF TRAINING & AWARENESS

9.1 Training Requirement

All employees of White Caves Real Estate LLC must complete mandatory AML/CFT training within 30 days of hire and annually thereafter. 100% staff participation is required.

9.2 Training Content

The annual training program shall cover:

  • Overview of UAE AML/CFT laws and regulations
  • Company AML/CFT policies and procedures
  • Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) procedures
  • Red flags and suspicious activity indicators
  • Transaction monitoring and reporting requirements
  • Prohibition on "tipping off" and consequences
  • Record keeping and documentation practices
  • Whistleblower protections and procedures
  • Case studies and real-world examples of AML/FT cases
  • Sanctions and consequences of non-compliance

9.3 Training Delivery

Training may be delivered through:

  • In-person workshops (preferred for comprehensive coverage)
  • Online training modules with quizzes
  • Video presentations
  • Combination of methods

All training must be documented with:

  • Attendance records (name, date, trainer)
  • Training materials and curriculum
  • Quiz or assessment results (if applicable)
  • Training certificate issued upon completion
  • Records retained for minimum 5 years

9.4 Role-Specific Training

The AML Compliance Officer shall receive advanced training covering:

  • Advanced money laundering typologies and techniques
  • goAML portal usage and reporting procedures
  • Investigations and STR preparation
  • Regulatory examination procedures
  • Sanctions compliance and PEP screening databases

9.5 Awareness Activities

Beyond formal training, ongoing awareness shall be maintained through:

  • Monthly compliance newsletters
  • Posters and visual reminders in office
  • Case studies shared during team meetings
  • Updates on new regulations and red flags
  • Compliance culture reinforcement from Senior Management

10. INTERNAL AUDIT & COMPLIANCE REVIEW

10.1 Audit Frequency & Scope

Internal compliance audits shall be conducted at least annually by either an internal audit team or an external independent auditor. The audit shall assess:

  • Adequacy and effectiveness of AML/CFT policies and procedures
  • Compliance with CDD and EDD requirements
  • Completeness of customer records and documentation
  • Transaction monitoring accuracy and STR filing timeliness
  • Staff training completion and effectiveness
  • Record retention and security measures
  • Compliance Officer authority and independence

10.2 Audit Report & Recommendations

The audit shall result in a formal report documenting:

  • Audit scope, methodology, and audit period
  • Findings and areas of deficiency
  • Recommendations for improvement
  • Auditor's overall compliance assessment
  • Management's response and action plan
  • Timeline for remediation of identified gaps

10.3 Reporting to Senior Management

The Compliance Officer shall report to Senior Management / Board monthly with:

  • Summary of transactions monitored
  • New customers accepted and risk classification
  • Any suspicious activity identified or STRs filed
  • Staff training completed
  • Compliance issues or regulatory communications received
  • Recommendations for policy improvements

10.4 Regulatory Inspection Readiness

White Caves Real Estate LLC shall maintain readiness for surprise inspections by DED or FIU examiners. All documentation should be organized and accessible. The Compliance Officer shall:

  • Designate an inspection coordinator
  • Ensure records are readily available
  • Prepare summary documentation
  • Conduct mock inspections annually
  • Maintain current policies and procedures

11. RISK ASSESSMENT

11.1 Risk Factors for White Caves Real Estate

As a real estate company, White Caves faces the following ML/FT risk factors:

  • Asset Laundering: Real estate properties are often used to obscure illicit origins of funds
  • High-Value Transactions: Large property values create opportunities for value transfer
  • International Clients: Foreign buyer base increases exposure to cross-border ML/FT
  • Cash Involvement: Deposits, down payments, and transaction costs may involve cash
  • Complex Structures: Use of corporate entities, trusts, and shell companies to conceal beneficial ownership
  • Developer Relationships: Relationships with contractors and developers increase third-party risk

11.2 Risk Mitigation Measures

To mitigate identified risks, White Caves implements:

  • Robust CDD and EDD procedures
  • Beneficial ownership verification for all corporate customers
  • Source of funds documentation for all transactions exceeding AED 5,000,000
  • PEP and sanctions screening for all customers
  • Transaction monitoring and STR filing procedures
  • Staff training on ML/FT indicators specific to real estate
  • Enhanced monitoring of high-risk jurisdictions and cash transactions
  • Cooperation with DED and FIU in compliance activities

11.3 Annual Risk Assessment

The Compliance Officer shall conduct an annual risk assessment to:

  • Identify new or emerging ML/FT risks
  • Evaluate effectiveness of current risk mitigation measures
  • Recommend updates to policies and procedures
  • Assess changes in customer base or transaction patterns
  • Document risk assessment findings and recommendations

12. PENALTIES & ENFORCEMENT

12.1 Employee Disciplinary Action

Violation of this AML/CFT Policy by any employee may result in:

  • Written warning for first offense
  • Suspension without pay for repeated violations
  • Termination of employment for serious breaches
  • Criminal prosecution for intentional violations or "tipping off"

12.2 Corporate Penalties

Non-compliance by the company may result in:

  • Administrative fines: AED 1,000,000 to AED 5,000,000
  • Suspension or revocation of commercial license
  • Seizure and forfeiture of suspicious assets
  • International sanctions and FATF grey-listing
  • Reputational damage and loss of business relationships

12.3 Individual Criminal Liability

Officers and employees responsible for compliance breaches may face:

  • Criminal prosecution and imprisonment up to 5 years
  • Fines up to AED 1,000,000
  • Disqualification from holding company positions
  • International travel restrictions

12.4 "Tipping Off" Criminal Penalties

The intentional disclosure that an STR has been filed or that a customer is suspected of ML/FT activities is a serious criminal offense with penalties including imprisonment and substantial fines. Any employee found guilty of "tipping off" will face:

  • Termination of employment
  • Immediate criminal referral to law enforcement
  • Prosecution and potential imprisonment
  • Civil liability for damages
