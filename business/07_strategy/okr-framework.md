## OKR Principles at White Caves

1. **Objectives** are ambitious, qualitative, inspiring goals
2. **Key Results** are measurable outcomes (not tasks or outputs)
3. Target attainment: 70% = success (100% means not ambitious enough)
4. Maximum 3–5 OKRs per department per quarter
5. Company OKRs cascade to department OKRs
6. Reviewed monthly; reset quarterly

## OKR Scoring

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 0.0–0.3 | Failed — missed, must investigate why |
| 0.4–0.6 | Progress made but below expectation |

```

Location: CRM Video Conference / Dubai Office
Facilitator: Managing Director (or @Margaret PM)
Note-taker: @Margaret

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
00:00  Opening — Facilitator reviews rules (5 min)
        • No blame, focus on learning
        • Score = progress toward outcome, not task completion
        • Red = need help, not failure

00:05  Company OKR Scorecard Review (15 min)
        • @Ada presents updated company OKR scores
        • Traffic light: GREEN (≥ 0.7) | YELLOW (0.4–0.69) | RED (< 0.4)

00:20  Department OKR Updates (25 min — 5 min per dept)
        Order: Sales → Marketing → Operations → Tech → Finance → Compliance
        Each department head presents:
          (a) Current score for each KR (0.0–1.0)
          (b) What changed since last month
          (c) One blocker or risk

00:45  Off-Track OKR Decision Tree (10 min)
        For each RED or YELLOW OKR:
          → Is the target still achievable this quarter? YES/NO
          → If YES: What specific action will change trajectory? (Name + deadline)
          → If NO: Should we adjust the KR target? (Requires MD approval)
          → If adjusted: Log adjustment in OKR History Table (see below)

00:55  Next Month Priorities (5 min)
        • Each department states their #1 priority for next 30 days
        • MD confirms alignment with company OKRs

01:00  Close
```

## OKR Anti-Patterns

> Common OKR mistakes — with White Caves-specific examples of what to avoid.

### Anti-Pattern 1: Output KRs Instead of Outcome KRs

**Definition:** A KR measures a task being done (output), not a result achieved (outcome).

| ❌ Anti-Pattern (Output KR) | ✅ Correct (Outcome KR) |
|-----------------------------|------------------------|
| "Launch landlord portal" | "5 landlords logging into portal weekly" |
| "Publish 3 blog articles" | "Blog articles drive 100 organic visitors each" |
| "Integrate Stripe payments" | "70% of tenants paying rent via Stripe (not cheque)" |
| "Build WhatsApp bot" | "Bot handles 60% of lead qualifications without human handoff" |
| "Deploy Arabic translation" | "30% of sessions use Arabic language" |

**White Caves Risk:** Phase 2 OKRs are particularly vulnerable to this — shipping the portal is measurable, but does NOT tell us if landlords actually use it.

---

### Anti-Pattern 2: Too Many OKRs

**Definition:** More than 3–5 OKRs per department, or more than 5 KRs per Objective.

**White Caves Specific Risk:** The Technology department currently has 5 KRs per Objective — borderline. If a new Phase 2 feature is added mid-quarter, the department will have 6+ active objectives and nothing will get full attention.

**Rule:** If you add a new OKR mid-quarter, retire an existing one or move it to the backlog.

---

### Anti-Pattern 3: Setting KRs to "1.0 Comfort Targets"

**Definition:** KRs are set at a level you're 100% confident of achieving — no stretch.

**Example of what NOT to do:**
- KR: "Test coverage: 7,800+ test cases" — we already have 7,744. That's 56 tests (1 sprint of work). Not ambitious.
- KR: "0 npm vulnerabilities" — if there are 7 already and a fix takes 1 day, this is a task, not a KR.

**Correct Approach:** KRs should make the team say "we can probably get 70% of the way there — it'll be tough." If the team says "easy," make it harder.

---

### Anti-Pattern 4: Not Reviewing Monthly

**Definition:** OKRs are set in Week 1 and not looked at until the quarterly review.

**White Caves Specific Risk:** The Sales OKR "5 closed deals by Q4 2026" has a 6-month horizon. Without monthly check-ins, the team may realise in Month 5 that they're at 1 deal — too late to course-correct.

**Rule:** Every OKR gets a score (0.0–1.0) at the first Monday of every month. If score isn't updated, the meeting doesn't proceed.

---

### Anti-Pattern 5: Cascading Tasks Down, Not Outcomes

**Definition:** Manager sets Company OKR → tells department to "deliver X feature" → department sets a task-based OKR instead of an outcome OKR.

**White Caves Example to Avoid:**
- Company OKR 2: Launch functional portals
- ❌ Wrong cascade: Tech OKR = "Complete all 8 backend stubs" (task)
- ✅ Right cascade: Tech OKR = "All 5 landlords can see real portfolio data in portal" (outcome)

---

### Anti-Pattern 6: OKRs Live Only in Documents

**Definition:** OKRs are written in a Google Doc and never referenced again.

**White Caves Rule:** Every sprint planning session MUST start with: "Which OKR does this sprint support?" If the answer is "none of them," deprioritise the sprint item. OKRs are the filter for prioritisation.
